Musical Impulses as of August 1st, 2026
My current musical impulses have perhaps not moved much in style, but there has been a slight shift to hypermodern, in that most of the music I have been currently listening to were published in the 2010’s or later. Can I help being in touch with the now and the current? I don’t look for what’s trendy, what’s trendy finds me, because I’m a magnet for the cool and eclectic. Them’s the rules.

Anyway, in the live music world, I saw Chinese American Bear at The Rebel Lounge. They dropped an album this year, Dim Sum & Then Some, which was very poppy, cheery, yet eclectic, different. Even compared to their other albums (I have only explored their entire discography recently), it sounds different, while still maintaining this childlike wonder that exists in their music. And because The Rebel Lounge had a great sale on tickets - $11 each – I had to go. 

I didn’t know anyone going, but that didn’t deter me – I’ve been doing the solo date thing before it was cool. I wore a nice dress and headed out past sunset, windows down, as usual. I was late enough to miss the openers, and I went straight to the bathroom to fix my hair, which was really awkward because I decided, for no particular reason, to go into the stall and set up my phone camera, and I fixed my hair and applied lip gloss and smoothed out my dress, in many panicked motions, and I think another girl must have saw me through the seams of the door, and who designs these stalls anyway, why does every stall in America have such a large gap between the door and the wall, have you ever sat on the toilet and made eye contact with someone at the sink through the mirror, and you think, this is me at my most vulnerable, skin to porcelain, bowl of still water waiting for waste, this is me about to empty myself, and now I am looking at you, a stranger, and it is so-, and that’s how it felt, when I walked out the stall and the girl said I looked nice, I felt see through and floating like a glass bird, and all I could say was thank you.

When I left the bathroom, I ran headfirst into Tristan – what a pleasant surprise! Sometimes I feel like I see him everywhere. Do you think he is spying on me? Anyway, I joined his group of friends, and the duo came on stage to rancorous cheers. What I know about Chinese American Bear is that they are a married duo making their own brand of indie pop music. Their chemistry was palpable – as if the romantic chemistry translated into a musical chemistry that transferred to the audience itself. They were in sync, bubbly, happy. Anne, who was the main vocalist and also played synths, had the energy of a kindergarten teacher, wearing a patterned smock dress and singing in a smiling higher octave. It was easy to get lost in this cheery energy, even with the contrastingly pessimistic lyrics: kids go down on the merry go round / 'Round and 'round the town / We go down, spinnin' round and around / Face down in the ground. I have to admit I was more enamored with Anne than her partner, Bryce, who had very full hair that Tristan and co. speculated was a wig. His guitar-ing was groovy, his tone was bright, poppy even, and he had an electric solo during one of the songs that captivated the crowd.

Really, my only issue with the band was their lack of bass guitar – not a lack, they just had an actual backing track playing: gunshot to my heart. Backing track to bass guitar is what Generative AI is to software engineers, simply lacking in the necessary human touch. A shame, as their basslines are an aspect of their music that drew me in, like in [song]. 

I also saw Meet me @ the Altar at The Rebel Lounge, also a win from the $11 sale. I think I got into MM@TA around covid times, and it was songs like Garden and May the odds be in your favor that drew me in, they had this hardness to them, an edge, while also being catchy. Aka, very well done pop punk. I’d seen them at Blueberry Hill in St. Louis some years back (has it really been years…?), and now they were touring in support of their new e.p. Worried Sick, which also had a few hits I was streaming heavily last year. At this show, the crowd was exceptionally short, maybe because it was an all-ages event. But the kids are alright – they were moshing, they were jumping, they were screaming their hearts out. MM@TA projects an inviting energy throughout, that everyone is welcome. At one point, Edith, vocals, open up the middle of the floor to a woman only mosh pit, a moment of unity, women of all ages and types spinning, at some point a woman’s wig fell off, she picked it up and waved it like a victory flag. Edith herself was my favorite part of the show, her vocals were so good, like really good, I want her to sing a Beyonce song, just punkified. I felt very happy to be there, happy to see a black girl (the same age as me!) on stage, rocking out.
