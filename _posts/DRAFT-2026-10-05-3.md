_i got dental implants in september_
My favorite part of the procedure was the sedation: the cool fluid entering my veins, the dizzy confusion of waking up an hour later, slumped in a chair. I kept staring at the window, the cars in the parking lot, and the little trees and bushes, trying to see the world straight, but everything moved like a wave. I fell asleep and woke up over and over again, in the same daze, like I was trapped in a dream. I will never drink again, but that was the closest I felt to being drunk in a long while. 

Overall, the procedure was not too bad. But I still took off ample time from work to recover. My sister flew in from Chicago to act as my emotional support human, driving me to and from the facility, and joining me for breakfast, lunch, and dinner for the next few days. Although I was still swollen, I had recovered some energy the next day, and we went to the Arabian library in Scottsdale to study. My face was still swollen, and I felt like an alien, so I wore a mask inside. I got some studying done, and also picked up a book about Smartphones and Doomscrolling. 

We went to In and Out later. I kept trying to tell my sister that it's not fine dining, but well marketed fast food, but she still ended up disappointed at the burger and fries she had. We also stopped by my favorite ice cream place, _Brewster's,_ and I was saddened to see that she was not at all impressed with their ice cream. I, however, was as happy as could be with my birthday day ice cream cup! 

The days moved fast. I had hoped to do something fun with my sister while she was in town, but the cards weren't in my favor. So, I went off on my own to the Kelela concert at the Van Buren, meeting up with V when I arrived. V is an intern from last year's cohort, who I've took upon myself to adopt. I think she has the demeaner of a 90's cartoon character, think MTV Downtown or Daria, effortlessly cool, but also very down to earth and inviting. What I have emphasized to V is this: You have 6 months of freedom before the real work kicks in, and suddenly you're dreaming about code and thinking in terms of fiscal year quarters and deadlines. Go outside, have fun, never be in the house. If you're bored, you're doing it wrong. I think they understood?

The concert itself was great. I was barely born when Aaliyah was at her peak popularity, but I've listened to all her music, and Kelela sounds like an extension of her. While her music probably more experimental, at times genre bending, electronic, pop, rock and more, the core soundscape is R&B, and she shows a true love for the art, giving it the respect it deserves. I felt bad for not knowing enough about her discography, so that's my homework. 

At the end of the week, we (Lanae, V, Maya, Phuong and I) gathered at Joyce's for her housewarming/karaoke night. Joyce, ever so generous, provided me with a cup of mac and cheese from _Gus's Fried Chicken,_ since I'm not supposed to do much chewing while I recover. Lanae tried a bite, and she did not like it at all, perhaps because it had a slightly artificial taste. I, however, love the artificial style mac and cheese, and I ate the whole cup with a smile on my face. 

Yes, I did sing _Sepsis,_ in which Maya expressed consternation at some of the lyrics, especially the line _the sex is almost always bad._ And I agree, nobody should accept sex that is almost always bad. But a song is a song, and even if I don't agree with the premise, I gave it justice (probably, maybe). It was a night with no cameras (besides my camcorder, but that's different!), no boys, no outside distractions, only the best people in the world.
