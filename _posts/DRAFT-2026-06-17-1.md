I always feel guilty when I check out too many books from the library. I know I shouldn't feel bad for using the library for its purpose, but I never read them all, and I get this nagging feeling that somebody's desperately waiting for whatever book(s) I have, and they're checking the library website every day, and they're seeing the book is checked out, and they're like, _Damn it, it has to be Erika, Erika checked out the book and I hate her, she will fall by my hand and my hand alone_, and they might be someone adept in the sword, and they'll take their sword and find me, cooped up in my apartment, books on the floor, and they'll challenge me to a dual, which of course I'd win, knowing that I am also adept in the sword, but it would all be so sad, an unnecessary tragedy, because no lost library book should result in violence. Still, none of this stopped me from checking out a pile of books when I meant to only pick up one, _Arrangements in Blue: Notes on Loving and Living Alone,_ which to my knowledge is about a single woman living (coping? understanding?) life as a single woman. Typically, I wouldn't read such a book as it sounds sappy, but I saw it mentioned in a review of bell hooks' novel _Communion: The Female Search for Love_ and I did like that one (besides the very long chapter on women "choosing" to be lesbian), so I decided to give it a looksee. To be clear, this post is not a longer reflection on singleness. On that, all I can say on that is (1) Touch grass. (2) Year of 6oyfriend is not over. As long as the fiscal/physical year is in session, there's still time to get physical. Get in the field!!

When I was a child, I went to Grande Prairie Public Library almost weekly. I don't think their website has changed in the past 10 years. It was a 2 floor library and I mostly sat downstairs in the kids area, although as I grew older I'd sometimes sit in the adult area and read or scroll Pokemon on my laptop. I had a bad habit of leaning in my chair which I'd always get yelled at for by an old lady named Beverly. But, she'd let me stay there for hours even though children weren't allowed to be there unsupervised - I once saw a brother and sister get kicked out for doing so. Sometimes I'd hang out with my elementary school best friend, who really I had a crush on, but I was in very deep denial. I mean, I'd put his name in the "does your crush love you" flash games, and I recall confessing on Gmail when Gmail had a messaging system (ha) and quickly saying nevermind. He was a handsome Jehovah's Witness so I never told him happy birthday. He had cute ears that were pierced (maybe?). We'd play Pokemon and Beyblade, and discuss those topics in depth. Sometime during 6th grade he started dating a pretty latina girl, which is also when we drifted apart. She has twins now (not his!).

What else from the library. My first real taste of independence was walking across the street from the library to aldi to pickup a huge bag of cheetos for 79 cents. Money i collected from lost quarters. those same chips have since double in price, call it inflation.

I'm hoping these books will restore my creative brain, although I know that's a big ask. Lately I've been trying to disconnect from the outside world and disappear into the page, away from thoughts, feelings, bodies, and other complications.
