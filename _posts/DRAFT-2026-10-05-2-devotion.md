What is the first thing you think about when you wake up in the morning? What is the last thing you think about at night? That is your devotion - what occupies your mind subconsciously. Wake up and work. Spend the day working, crafting, perfecting. Spend the nights dreaming of more. Seriously. What are you willing to give for a dream? What are you willing to sacrifice?

It is of upmost importance that I immerse myself in the output of creatives. It's why I'm constantly listening to music or reading - by internalizing the fruits of other's labor, I hope that it will propel me to enter my own state of creativity, to produce admirable work, any work actually, nobody has to like it, but it has to exist for anyone to form an opinion at all. The hardest part is getting starting, forcing myself past my own inhibitions and hesitations - will this writing be good, will it be worth it at all - questions that I ignore, put pen to paper. The 2nd hardest part is finishing. I can start drafts upon drafts, but crafting a body until it stands on its on is where I find trouble. Yes, that is why I declared October, Blogtober. Pretend it's still Blogtober.

_[Sometime in September]_

I understand theatre kids now, the memorization of the songs, the constant outbursts of music, cosplay, long Reddit threads, general obsession. I understand it all after seeing _Les Misérables_ at the Phoenix Theatre Company. This time, I went with Johnny and his friend Jean to the production. On the stage, I was witness to the dedication to perfection, watching the actors transition between pivotal moments through song and dance. The highlight to me was Éponine's heartache - a woman written to be destroyed by unfulfilled desire. It's not often that woman are written to lose - usually it's men we see stuck in the "friendzone." I was happy/sad to see Éponine pine endlessly, and ultimately, <details><summary> Spoilers! die without achieving romantic love. Happy because I love to see woman characters experience emotions that are relatable. Sad, because to see her demise on the stage in the arms of Marius hurt my heart, she was loved by him, but it would never be in the way she wanted - did this make her selfish, to ask for something he couldn't give? The musical always ends the same, with her broken heart. </summary></details> Poor Éponine! 

My favorite actors/actresses of the night were Tre Moore as Marius, whose carefully portrayed the dueling love he shared for Cosette and Éponine. Also, Ethan Rogers as Jean Valjean, who looked good... but also portrayed Valjean with the humility and courage that is present in his character.

I haven't seen a musical since high school maybe, and I was nervous I wouldn't know what was going on, but I kept up just fine. The orchestra, barely visible under the stage deck, was immersive and cascading, and even though I couldn't understand every lyric, the emotions of each scene were easy to discern. I probably should not have gotten up at 4am that day though!

-

After seeing Les Misérables, I stopped by my house and then headed to Glendale for Maya's pole dance recital. Like the production of Les Misérables, the recital laid out a stage and a cast of characters using their bodies to tell a story. And you could see from their movements the level of devotion required to twist and turn through the complex choreographies created for the performance. One woman performed under an alter ego. _In the daytime,_ the announcer said, _she's a mother and a nurse. But here, she's-_ I don't recall the name of the alter ego. But that's besides the point. Another woman, probably the oldest of the performers, came on stage with a mean pixie and black lipstick. She glowed under the lights and moved with precision and unshakeable confidence, but as she motioned to remove her overcoat, her zipper got stuck, and she ended up starting the routine over. For a moment, she looked shaken, maybe annoyed at how quickly the dance she had crafted was derailed. But when the music restarted, she snapped back into focus, and this time, how overcoat slipped off with ease, as if thrusted by the wind. 

When Maya performed, I again could understand the lure of the pole. The way you could manipulate physics, bend bodies, build momentum, and propel yourself - I could tell, although her routine was carefully put together, there was a sense a freedom evoked, no bashfulness, no shyness, no hiding, just a real body in motion. Maya's routine was one only she could do, a fusion of Jamaican and Black culture, along with her own sense of style. There were elements of her dancing that I'd seen glimpses of when we'd went out to clubs, and gravity defying motions on the pole, when she pushed herself upwards and contorted in a way that defied gravity. The work that she had put in, for many months, all came together that night, and yet, she made it look effortless. And that is the art of devotion.

Afterwards, Maya, Lanae, Joyce, and I took a group picture and headed out to _Thirsty Lion_ for a celebratory dinner. Filling my car was a song I had been listening to some years ago, that had randomly come to mind: _get out of my head._ Immediately, I searched for meaning? The lyrics reverberating through the speakers, _Last night I had another dream about you / How many fuckin' dreams must I have about you? / Moving on, trying hard to get away / From your face, your taste,_ what does it all say about me? That I think of this song now? As an (amateur) litterateur and software engineer, analysis is my daily state of being. The curtains are never just blue. But what if the curtains didn't exist at all? What if I could be free of labor of thinking, and, like bodies swirling on metal, flow freely?

_Some days later..._

Today, we phenomenal Black women (Joyce, Maya, Lanae, and I) moved the last of Joyce's belongings to her new apartment. The laborious task was an exercise in using our bodies for practical purposes, through sweat, through tears, through the hot (but not too hot - I love how Phoenix dips lightly into fall. Those low 90's feel blissful, and I will argue with no one about this.) sun stabbing our skin. In the middle of moving, a nice man with a sparkling stud earring, fresh haircut, and tattoos generously assisted us with lifting some furniture into the truck. It seemed he had just went on a lunch run, and Lanae guarded his Subway sandwich with her life. Did I ask "smash or pass" in the elevator as he left? Maybe so! Did he hear? Hopefully not! Maya drove the moving truck to the new apartment, and once we finally moved everything from truck to garage, Lanae and I moped on the couch, waiting for Maya, who took off to retrieve her misplaced phone, and Joyce, who was returning the moving truck. We had worked so hard and time was moving so slowly, hunger was eating us alive, it seemed like the minutes had turned into days, and there was nothing left to do but lament the emptiness we felt, until finally the girls returned, and we went down to Mesa for a masterful dinner. We sat at a Chinese restaurant in Mesa's Mekong Plaza, where I ordered a platter of noodles that was an absurd portion. The experience felt oddly nostalgic, but I brushed off those wispy feelings. Then, we went to a shaved ice cream shop for dessert, where we discussed who was the most neurodivergent in our group. Regardless of any complaints or disagreements, I have decided that it is me!

On the way back, Maya and I had a casual conversation on the comings on goings of our lives. She mentioned that her wisdom teeth needed to be extracted soon. The strange happenings of the human body amused me. We could dance, we could lift heavy objects, and sometimes we would wake up with extra teeth we didn't need. Sometimes you could feel so in control, and sometimes your body was just a shell that you were occupying, a room to sleep in. My body had worked hard today. In return, I fed it overly greasy Chinese food. Balance is key.
