Around the world, English teachers' (link the band?) are telling their students "Don't begin your essays with _'Since the dawn of time'_" or other lofty variations. But it's simply a fact that _since the dawn of time_, the Earth has rotated on its axis at a speed of [speed] miles per hours, and has orbited around the sun at a of [speed] miles per hour, a constant motion, not detectable with our own senses, but a movement we can observe in the rising and lowering of the sun and the shifting of the seasons. Therefore, it is completely normal to feel, at times, as if you are spinning through life, as in one way, it's true.

Now, I present to you the MERRY-GO-ROUND problem:
You are placed on a MERRY-GO-ROUND that cannot be stopped by any external or internal forces. In other words, it is an endlessly spinning machine. The MERRY-GO-ROUND rotates at such a speed that there is a 50% chance of survival if you were to jump off. Do you:

(A) Remain on the ride
(B) Jump
(C) ???

And why?

For those that choose A: How do you imagine the rest of your life unfolds if you remain on the ride?
For those that choose B: Do you survive? Why or why not?
For those that choose C: Elaborate.

The common answer: Jump! Even when I put more restraints on the result (if you jump and perish, it will be a prolonged and painful death. If you remain on the ride, your family will join you. If you remain on the ride, you'll live to old age with pristine health), nobody wanted to stay on the ride. For many, to stay in a state of spin is to relinquish control over the outcome of your life, and overall, seems quite boring and impractical. Except Kobe, who says, "letting go and letting some external stimuli direct you feels kind of sickening but at the same time, seductive."

I felt the same. There can be no stress when you're constantly spinning. Why deal with the traumas of the outside world when you can enclose yourself in a safe space, a carnival ride with no end? And I don't think I'm brave enough to be a jumper, although some have disagreed. Some say I do have the tendencies of a jumper.

A lot of music uses repetition, the feeling of spinning, as a means of implying something sinister. The same hook repeated over and over has an eerie feeling to it - as if something is broken, the clock is stuck in its place, and even if the words themselves are joyful, the consistent motions of the lyrics and vocals and riffs feels suffocating.

There's an idiom that many people use, for example, "I feel like I'm just spinning my wheels," meaning "[wasting] time doing things that achieve nothing." That's the essence of the MERRY-GO-ROUND problem. You can spin forever, and live a long life. But you'll have no legacy (besides holding the world record for longest time spent continously on a MERRY-GO-ROUND.).
