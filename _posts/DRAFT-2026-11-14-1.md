Firstly, because hanging out with my friends is always worthwile, and second, because I sense that I am becoming a homebody in a not good way - like I'm so afraid of "wasting" time, or breaking my routine, that I don't go anywhere, when really, it's not like I would be doing anything important at this hour, and there's a whole weekend ahead of me that I can spend doing whatever I like.

11-14-2026
This might be my apology post to Johnny. Today was supposed to be perfect - I was going to be on time for his birthday celebration. Unfortunately, timing was not on my side. Let me explain: I had therapy today, and I had pushed back the session to 10am. I was supposed to go to Discount Tire beforehand, get some air in my tires, go to therapy, then the mini-golf, then the BBQ. But, I underestimated how much time it would take to do my morning workout, shower, and get ready. I complained about it to my therapist - _why can't I be on time to things?_ Timeliness is something that can be worked on, she said, but you can't keep downing yourself because you're late. You arrive when you arrive. I've realized that I imagine the disappointed faces of my friends at my tardiness before I even arrive, and that brings about much unnecessary stress and anxiety. So I tried to focus on the truth of the matter: I arrive when I arrive. I went to Discount Tire after therapy, hoping it would be a 5 minute trip, but there was such a long line, and I realized: this has to stop. I am going to order a pump off of Amazon, and fill my tires myself. I learned I needed an alignment (this is a reminder to myself).

I left Discount Tired and it was already noon. Mini golf had started at 11. I was on the 101 and then the i17, and then I had to exit the i17 because it was partially closed. I decided I was tired of highway driving and was determined to take a local route, which in hindsight was an unneccessary move, because it added another 10 minutes to my commute. Lucky me, but they were still mini golfing when I arrived. I wasn't too interested in golfing myself, but I did want to hang out with the group, but before I could do that, I had to eat - I was horrifically hungry. I went inside and purchased an order of fries, and waited, and waited. Eventually the cashier told me that the "machine" was "broken" - God knows how you break a fryer - and I should go to the other food court across the way to retrieve my fries. What should have been a quick break now involved a trek across the park, further delaying my arrival to the mini golfing.

I walked past the single functional rollercoaster and the merry go round to the other food court. I explained the situation and a worker handed me a cup of fries. Hardly enough fries for $6, but I was hungry and held my complaints. I didn't want to walk and eat so I sat down and enjoyed my fries with a little ketchup. The fries themselves were good, shoestring style, probably the same size as McDonald's fries, but crispier. I just wish they had more sauces available.

Once I was done I made my way to the golf course. Because one of the workers mentioned I needed a club to go to the courses, I purchased one, which I immediately realized was a mistake because I wasn't playing and the whole park is run by disassociated teenagers who could care less about whether someone has a golf club on the mini golf course. But hey, money comes, and money goes. I'll survive.

I finally found Johnny and co as they neared the end of their game, and Johnny was winning by what appeared to be a lot. I did like the course design, there was some cool bridges over the bodies of water and well painted fixtures of restaurants and houses and whatnot, and it looked deceptively easy. One of Johnny's friend's was wearing this oversized gray sweater and cargos, and I immediately took that as a sign to bring back cargos into my life. Over the years I had a feminine urge to rid myself of most of my baggy pants in exchange for skinny jeans and miniskirts and bell bottoms, all of which I love, but now I see I can be feminine and wear cargos, and I also think it would fit this "grunge" aesthetic that I've been attracted to, so thank you Johnny's friend, for that inspiration. After mini golf, the group headed to Little Miss Barbecue in Sunnyslope. When I arrived, I parked my car, but I had to put together Johnny's gift, which took longer than expected, and by the time I was walking out there was a line out the door. It was a Texas style BBQ restaurant, and I had hoped it wouldn't take too long to cut up some meat and get everyone seated, but I think I spent a while in the line, because by the time I retrieved my food, paid, and sat down, the group was gone, and I felt horrendously terrible for my tardiness. But, I didn't regret holding my spot in the line, because although I got a simple platter of turkey breast and mac and cheese, it had to have been the best turkey breast I've ever had - perfect cut, tender, juicy, and bursting with warm flavor that worked well with the spicy and tangy barbecue sauces sitting on the table. Ironically, I was reading the book _Butter,_ in which the main character was describing herself realising her inhibitions and simply enjoying good tasting food for the sake of it, and I felt in that moment I was having the same realization. Satisfied, I texted Johnny with my deepest apologies, and headed to Provision coffee to meet Lanae and Joyce and catch up on some blog posts (and write this one).
